Fund It or Kill It

Positive-NPV rule · capital rationing

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Six proposals, one budget: which projects deserve scarce capital, and what happens to unspent riyals?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Only returns above the cost of capital create value.
  • When capital is scarce, rank by value per scarce unit.
  • A full budget is not success; return unspent cash.

🖼️The picture

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Judge whether a project clears the cost of capital.
  • Weigh projects by value per unit of scarce resource.
  • Explain why idle cash drags value; returned cash does not.

💡The idea

Picture a high-jump bar set at the cost of capital. Each project’s return is its jump. Clearing the bar by a metre beats a centimetre. Knocking it down costs points. A shiny HQ tower returning 3% never leaves the ground.

The bar is WACC: the blended return lenders and shareholders expect. Only returns above it create value. When good projects outnumber money or people, rank by value per unit of the scarcer one. Unspent cash should go back to owners, not sit idle.

🧭Decide like a CFO: Big bet or spread slate?

The budget is SAR 420M but your team can deliver only a few projects. Four clear the hurdle; the best is a large acquisition. The CEO also wants a flagship HQ.

Fund everything above the hurdle → over budget and team capacity; delivery slips and value leaks.

Acquisition plus digital twin → most value within both limits, but concentrated on one big bet.

Add the HQ to keep the CEO happy → profitable on paper, destroys value every year.

The call. Fund the acquisition and digital twin, refuse the HQ, and return unspent cash. Stress-test the acquisition forecast before committing.

🧮The formula, in plain words

Value a project adds each year (EVA)

EVA = (Return − WACC) × Capital invested

A project adds value only by the margin it beats the cost of capital, on every riyal used.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Funding a below-hurdle project because it is “strategic”.

✓ Name the option it buys; otherwise it destroys value every year.

✗ Funding down the list until the money runs out.

✓ Check every limit, including people, and compare complete slates.

✗ Spending the whole budget because it was approved.

✓ Idle cash drags returns and tempts waste; return what you cannot invest well.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1The CEO wants a profitable flagship HQ returning well below WACC, “to show we are serious”. Best CFO response?

Profit is not enough. Every year the HQ earns less than its capital costs, so shareholders are poorer.

2After funding every project above the hurdle, cash is left. The only other proposal returns just below WACC. Best use?

Returning is neutral. The proposal destroys value; idle cash drags returns and tempts waste.

3Your budget fits either one large project or two smaller ones that together add more value. Which do you choose?

Under rationing, maximise total value from the budget, not the size of any one project.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Every yes is a no elsewhere. The best slate may concentrate risk on one big bet. Returning cash shrinks the company; you may need to raise it again.

🧩 When the rule breaks

The rule is only as good as the forecasts, and acquisition forecasts are often the most optimistic. Riskier projects deserve higher hurdles. A small below-hurdle pilot can be a valuable option.

🏢 In real companies

Companies use an investment committee and release money in stages, so weak cases stop early. Vision 2030 has created many Saudi opportunities, so discipline matters most in boom years. The dollar peg means Saudi hurdles track US rates.

💼 In an interview

“You have five good projects but money for two. How do you choose?”

First, only projects that clear our hurdle rate — the WACC adjusted for risk — create value; anything below destroys it however strategic it sounds. Then, because capital is rationed, I rank by value created per riyal invested, not by size or by who argues loudest. If nothing clears the hurdle, returning cash to shareholders beats a value-destroying trophy project.

📖 Key terms
WACC
The blended return lenders and shareholders expect: what capital costs.
Hurdle rate
Minimum return a project must earn; higher for riskier projects.
Capital rationing
More value-creating projects than money or people to fund them.
