The Refinancing Wall

Term structure · refinancing & duration risk

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Heavy floating debt and a maturity wall: how do you de-risk before rates jump?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Cheap debt at today’s rate can be dangerous tomorrow.
  • Your real exposure is what re-prices in twelve months.
  • Hedging is insurance: judge it by the storm it survives.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Explain how a rate rise reaches cover and rating.
  • Spot how much debt re-prices within a year.
  • Weigh cutting debt, fixing rates and refinancing early.

💡The idea

Picture three households: one on a long fixed-rate mortgage, one on a variable rate, and one on a variable rate with a fixed deal ending next year and falling income. The third is a company before a rate shock.

Floating debt, priced at SAIBOR plus a margin, re-prices within months; maturing debt must be replaced, maybe at a wider spread. Higher interest cuts cover, the rating slips and WACC rises. Three defences cost money in calm weather: cut debt, fix rates, refinance early.

🧭Decide like a CFO: The wall meets the storm

Debt is high and 85% re-prices within a year: most is floating, and a large fixed loan matures next year. Rates look set to rise and lenders are turning cautious.

Do nothing → no cost today, but a shock lifts interest sharply and can cost a rating notch.

Swap part to fixed, extend the maturity early → small yearly premium; shock’s extra interest roughly halved.

Cut debt → strongest protection, but you give up tax shield and may sell assets or dilute owners.

The call. Hedge and refinance now; cut debt towards the mid-range over time. The premium is insurance; judge it by the storm it survives.

🧮The formula, in plain words

Debt re-pricing within 12 months

Re-pricing share = (floating debt + fixed debt maturing within 12 months) ÷ total debt

The share of your debt that next year’s rates and spreads will reach.

🎮How it plays in the game

  • You start tall: the debt slider opens at 64%, rated B in calm weather, CCC in a storm.
  • Floating debt and maturities fold into leverage; the storm adds 50–150 bp by difficulty and costs a notch.
  • Cutting into the 30s or 40s earns an A in either weather; staying at 64% is a D.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Waiting until the maturity year to refinance

✓ Refinance when you can, not when you must.

✗ Stress-testing the benchmark rate but not the spread

✓ In a shock, rates rise and spreads widen together; test both.

✗ Treating hedging as a bet on rates

✓ Set a policy: a target fixed share and a cap on yearly maturities.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1Same debt: one firm is fixed for seven years, the other mostly floating with a maturity next year. Who is exposed?

Fixed debt holds its rate. The floating firm re-prices its floating debt and must refinance the maturity at new rates.

2A large bond matures in 14 months. Markets are open at an acceptable spread, but rates are volatile. What is good practice?

Refinancing risk is about access as much as price. A revolving facility just turns long-term debt into short, floating debt.

3A CFO swapped floating debt to fixed. A year later rates have fallen. How should the board judge the decision?

A hedge is insurance, not a forecast. Paying more after rates fall is the premium for certainty.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Every defence costs money when no storm comes: lost tax shield, fixed rates usually above floating, a premium for longer money. Not acting is a bet that rates and lenders stay kind.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Floating debt is not always wrong: revenues that rise with rates are a natural hedge. Treasurers hedge to a policy range, not everything. In a crisis, nobody may lend at any rate.

🏢 In real companies

With the riyal pegged to the dollar, SAMA generally follows US rates; many Saudi loans are priced at SAIBOR plus a margin. When US rates jumped from 2022, floating-rate GCC borrowers felt it within months; fixed-rate sukuk issuers were shielded.

💼 In an interview

“Rates are rising and a large loan matures next year. How do you manage the risk?”

Start early. I map the maturity wall and our floating-rate exposure, then stress-test interest cover and the rating under higher rates. Options: refinance or extend before the window tightens, swap part of the floating debt to fixed, and reduce leverage if the stress case breaches covenants. Debt that is cheap at today’s rate can be dangerous at tomorrow’s, so I price the downside, not the base case.

📖 Key terms
Floating-rate debt
Debt whose rate resets with a benchmark like SAIBOR, plus a margin.
Refinancing wall
A cluster of debt falling due in a short period.
Interest-rate swap
A contract exchanging floating payments for fixed ones, or the reverse.
