The Refinancing Wall

Term structure · refinancing & duration risk

6 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

How to reshape heavy, mostly floating debt before a rate shock and a cluster of maturities re-price it at once.

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Debt that is cheap at today’s rate can be dangerous at tomorrow’s.
  • Your real exposure is whatever re-prices in the next twelve months.
  • Refinance when you can, not when you must.
  • Hedging is insurance: judge it by the storm it survives.

💡The idea

Picture three households. One has a long fixed-rate mortgage; rate rises pass it by. One has a variable rate and feels every rise. The third has a variable rate, a fixed deal ending next year and a falling income. That third household is a company with floating debt and a wall of loans falling due.

Floating debt, priced at SAIBOR (the Saudi interbank rate) plus a margin, re-prices every few months; maturing debt must be replaced, maybe at a wider spread. Higher interest cuts cover, the rating slips and WACC rises. Three defences, each paid for in calm weather: cut debt, fix rates with swaps, or refinance early and stagger maturities.

🧮The maths

Extra interest from a rate rise

ΔInterest ≈ (floating debt + debt refinanced in the year) × Δr

floating debt
debt whose rate resets with a benchmark like SAIBOR
debt refinanced in the year
fixed debt that matures and must be replaced
Δr
benchmark rise plus any wider spread on new debt

Debt re-pricing within 12 months

Re-pricing share = (floating debt + fixed debt maturing within 12 months) ÷ total debt

re-pricing share
the part of debt exposed to next year’s rates

✍️Worked example: The wall meets the storm

EBIT is SAR 400M. Debt is SAR 2,400M: SAR 1,440M floating at 6.0% and SAR 960M fixed at 5.0%, of which SAR 600M matures next year. Then rates rise 200 bp and lenders add 50 bp of spread.

The working, step by step
  1. Today: interest = 1,440 × 6.0% + 960 × 5.0% = SAR 134.4M; cover ≈ 3.0×. Re-pricing share = 2,040 ÷ 2,400 = 85%.
  2. Do nothing: floating costs 1,440 × 2.0% = SAR 28.8M more; the maturity rolls from 5.0% to 7.5%: 600 × 2.5% = SAR 15.0M.
  3. Unhedged interest = 134.4 + 28.8 + 15.0 = SAR 178.2M; cover = 400 ÷ 178.2 ≈ 2.2×. In game bands, BB falls to B.
  4. Hedge now: swap SAR 720M to fixed at 6.5% (+SAR 3.6M); extend the maturity at 5.75% (+SAR 4.5M). Total SAR 8.1M.
  5. After the shock only SAR 720M re-prices: +SAR 14.4M. Hedged interest = 134.4 + 8.1 + 14.4 = SAR 156.9M; cover ≈ 2.5×.

Answer. Unhedged: +SAR 43.8M, cover 2.2×. Hedged: +SAR 22.5M, cover about 2.5×. If rates never rise, SAR 8.1M a year is the insurance premium.

  • You start tall: the debt slider opens at 64% — rated B in calm weather, CCC in a storm. Leaving it there is a D.

  • Cutting into the 30s or 40s earns an A in either weather (best: mid-40s calm, about 30% stormy). The high 50s score B at best.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Waiting until the year a loan matures to refinance

✓ Refinance when you can, not when you must: start well ahead of the wall.

✗ Stress-testing the benchmark rate but not the spread

✓ In a shock, rates rise and spreads widen together; test cover against both.

✗ Treating hedging as a bet on where rates go

✓ Set a policy — a target fixed-rate share, a cap on yearly maturities — and stick to it.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1Company A has SAR 1,000M fixed for seven years. Company B has SAR 800M floating and SAR 200M fixed, maturing next year. Rates rise 200 bp. Ignoring spreads, what extra interest?

A’s fixed rate holds. B pays 800 × 2% = SAR 16M, plus 200 × 2% = SAR 4M on the maturity: SAR 20M. The SAR 16M answer forgets refinancing.

2A SAR 900M bond matures in 14 months. Markets are open today at an acceptable spread, but rates are volatile. What is good practice?

Refinancing risk is about access as much as price. Paying a little more buys certainty; a revolving facility just turns long-term debt into short, floating debt.

3A CFO swaps SAR 500M of floating debt to fixed. A year later, rates have fallen 100 bp. Which statement is right?

500 × 1% = SAR 5M a year. A hedge is insurance, not a forecast; judged by hindsight, every policy looks wrong in a year without a claim.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Every defence costs money when no storm comes. Cutting debt gives up tax shield. Fixed rates usually cost more, and you keep paying if rates fall. Longer money costs a premium. The alternative is betting that rates and lenders stay kind when you need them.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Floating debt is not always wrong: a business whose revenues rise with rates has a natural hedge. Hedging everything looks foolish if rates fall, so treasurers hedge to a policy range. And in a crisis the problem may be that nobody lends at any rate.

🏢 In real companies

With the riyal pegged to the dollar, SAMA (the central bank) generally follows US rates, and many Saudi loans are priced at SAIBOR plus a margin. When US rates jumped from 2022, floating-rate GCC borrowers felt it within months; fixed-rate sukuk issuers were shielded until refinancing. Profit-rate swaps hedge Islamic financing.

💼 In an interview

“Rates are rising and a large loan matures next year. How do you manage the risk?”

Start early. I map the maturity wall and our floating-rate exposure, then stress-test interest cover and the rating under higher rates. Options: refinance or extend before the window tightens, swap part of the floating debt to fixed, and reduce leverage if the stress case breaches covenants. Debt that is cheap at today’s rate can be dangerous at tomorrow’s, so I price the downside, not the base case.

📖 Key terms
Floating-rate debt
Debt whose rate resets regularly with a benchmark like SAIBOR, plus a margin.
Refinancing wall
A cluster of debt falling due in a short period.
Interest-rate swap
A contract exchanging floating payments for fixed ones, or the reverse.
Credit spread
The margin paid above the benchmark; it widens as credit weakens.
Basis point (bp)
One hundredth of a percentage point: 100 bp = 1%.
