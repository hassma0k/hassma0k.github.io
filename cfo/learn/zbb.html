Cut Without Cutting Muscle

Zero-based budgeting (ZBB)

6 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Cut cost by rebuilding each budget from zero, and stop before a cut starves the revenue it protects.

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Cut the dead wood, not the branches that bear fruit.
  • A cut's real value is cash freed minus margin lost.
  • Where you cut matters more than how much.
  • Damage past the floor accelerates: spread the cuts and stop at the floor.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Explain how zero-based budgeting differs from trimming last year's budget.
  • Calculate a cut's real value: cash freed minus margin put at risk.
  • Shape cuts so low-value spend goes first and no revenue team passes its floor.

💡The idea

Think of pruning a fruit tree. Cutting dead wood makes the tree stronger. Cutting live branches loses next year's fruit. Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) is the pruning saw: every budget starts at zero and each activity must justify its cost by the value it delivers. Brilliant on dead wood, dangerous on live branches.

Every team has a floor: the deepest cut it can take before losing work that defends revenue. Above it sit duplicated reports and unused licences. Past it sit the campaigns and sales coverage that win next year's orders. Damage past the floor accelerates, so several shallow cuts, each stopping at its floor, beat one deep cut.

🧮The maths

Net value of a cut

Net value = Cash freed − Margin lost

Cash freed
spend removed from the budget each year
Margin lost
contribution margin on revenue the cut puts at risk

Margin lost

Margin lost = Revenue lost × Contribution margin %

Revenue lost
sales lost because the spend behind them was cut
Contribution margin %
what each riyal of sales leaves after variable costs

✍️Worked example: Same SAR 60M, two savings

Controllable cost is SAR 400M: G&A 80, Sales & Marketing (S&M) 120, Operations 96, R&D 64, IT 40. The CEO wants SAR 60M (15%) out. Floors, the deepest safe cut: G&A 34%, S&M 22%, IT 18%. Contribution margin: 35%.

The working, step by step
  1. Plan A: G&A 10% × 80 = 8, IT 10% × 40 = 4, S&M 40% × 120 = 48. Total 60.
  2. S&M's floor is 22% × 120 = 26.4, so 48 − 26.4 = 21.6 cuts campaigns and account coverage.
  3. Lost coverage costs SAR 140M revenue. Margin lost = 140 × 35% = 49. Real gain = 60 − 49 = 11.
  4. Plan B: G&A 34% × 80 = 27.2, S&M 22% × 120 = 26.4, IT 16% × 40 = 6.4. Total 60.
  5. No team passes its floor, so margin lost ≈ 0. Plan B is worth 60 − 11 = 49 more a year.

Answer. Choose Plan B. Where you cut matters more than how much: cut deepest where spend defends least revenue, and stop revenue teams at their floor.

🎮How it plays in the game

  • Five sliders, one per department, each cutting 0–40%. Markers show safe floors: G&A 34%, Sales & Marketing 22%, Operations 15%, R&D 10%, IT 18%.
  • Cuts up to the floor free cash and lift NOPAT one for one. Past it, 'starved margin' grows with the square of the overshoot.
  • Leaving the budget untouched earns a D. An A takes each team to about its floor, keeping 'Cash freed' high and 'Starved margin' near zero.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Cutting every department by the same percentage.

✓ Rank spend by the value it defends; cut deepest where that value is lowest.

✗ Counting the gross saving and ignoring margin at risk.

✓ Net off lost sales × contribution margin before approving any cut.

✗ Treating ZBB as a one-off purge.

✓ Give each cost an owner and track it quarterly so costs do not creep back.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1The CEO wants a 15% cost cut. Which approach is genuinely zero-based?

Zero-based means spend must prove its value. Equal cuts remove muscle and fat alike; a freeze only delays costs; size is not slack.

2Sales & Marketing spends SAR 120M; its floor is a 22% cut. Going to 30% saves SAR 9.6M more but loses SAR 40M of revenue at 35% margin. Profit change?

Margin lost = 40 × 35% = 14; net = 9.6 − 14 = −4.4. −30.4 subtracts revenue, not margin; −14 forgets the saving.

3You need SAR 10M more. X: cut Operations 10 more points; it is already 10 points past its floor. Y: take it from G&A and IT, both short of their floors. Which?

Each point past a floor starves more revenue than the last (the game squares the overshoot). 'Already done' is the sunk-cost trap: further cuts add new damage.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Every riyal cut removes capability. A deep cut's cost shows up two or three quarters later: a thinner pipeline, slower launches, customers who quietly leave. ZBB also costs management time, so many firms fully rebuild only every few years.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Floors are estimates, and every owner calls their spend muscle. Marketing pays back in months, R&D in years, so annual reviews over-cut long-horizon spend. In a cash crisis, cutting past floors can be right. In fast growth, reinvest freed cash in revenue teams.

🏢 In real companies

ZBB is common in consumer goods, retail and shared services, often after a margin squeeze. In the Gulf, cost drives often follow an oil-price fall. Two cautions: cutting Saudi roles can hurt Saudisation (Nitaqat) standing, affecting visas; and the richest savings usually sit in third-party spend such as consultants, travel and duplicated software.

💼 In an interview

“The CEO wants a 15% cost cut. How would you approach it?”

Zero-based: rebuild each budget from zero and rank every riyal by the value it defends, instead of trimming everything by 15%. That frees the most cash from low-value spend. But I would protect the floor of revenue-generating teams — cutting sales or product below that point starves the growth that funds the business. Deep cuts where value is low, light ones where it is high.

📖 Key terms
Zero-based budgeting (ZBB)
Building each budget from zero; every activity must justify its cost.
Controllable opex
Costs management can change within the year, such as admin, marketing, IT.
Floor
The deepest cut a team can take before losing revenue-defending work.
Contribution margin
Revenue minus variable costs: what each riyal of sales leaves over.
Cost creep
Costs drifting back after a cut, as old habits return.
