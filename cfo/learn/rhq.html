The 30-Year Tax Holiday

Effective tax rate · market access (Saudi RHQ programme)

5 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Decide whether, and which functions, to base in a Saudi Regional HQ: tax holiday and contract access versus higher costs.

⚡In 30 seconds

  • A tax holiday is only a saving if you can afford the address.
  • The incentive covers qualifying activities, not the whole group.
  • Often it is the contract door, not the tax rate, that carries the case.
  • Move functions one by one, on their own numbers.

🎯What you’ll learn

💡The idea

Think of a club membership with a discount and an entrance fee. The discount (a 30-year 0% tax holiday) applies only to qualifying RHQ activities, not the whole group. The membership card also opens a door: government contracts. The fee is real: higher running costs, relocation and local hiring.

A licensed RHQ gets 0% corporate and withholding tax on qualifying activities, but needs real substance: senior people running the region from Riyadh. Since 2024, many government contracts are closed to multinationals without one. The HQ must move to earn status; other functions move only if their own numbers work. Often access, not tax, carries the case.

🧮The maths

Annual value of an RHQ

Net value = Tax saved + Contract margin − Extra running cost − Relocation capital × WACC − Localisation cost

Contract margin
margin on bids × realistic win probability
Relocation capital × WACC
yearly cost of the money spent moving
Localisation cost
local hiring beyond what you would spend anyway

Tax saved

Tax saved = Qualifying profit × (Alternative tax rate − RHQ rate)

Qualifying profit
profit from activities the incentive covers
Alternative tax rate
rate that profit pays where it sits today

✍️Worked example: Is the move worth it?

Regional HQ profit of SAR 180M is taxed at 9% today and would all qualify for 0%. RHQ-only government contracts offer SAR 100M margin at a 40% win chance. Riyadh adds SAR 18M running cost, SAR 60M relocation capital (WACC 10%) and SAR 4M localisation.

The working, step by step
  1. Tax saved: 180 × (9% − 0%) = 16.2. Expected contracts: 100 × 40% = 40.
  2. Costs: running 18, relocation 60 × 10% = 6, localisation 4.
  3. Net value: 16.2 + 40 − 18 − 6 − 4 = 28.2 a year.
  4. Without contracts: 16.2 − 18 − 6 − 4 = −11.8. The tax holiday alone does not pay.
  5. Break-even win rate: 11.8 ÷ 100 = 11.8%.

Answer. Move the HQ: about SAR 28.2M a year. But government work, not tax, carries the case; it still breaks even at an 11.8% win rate.

🎮How it plays in the game

  • Place Regional HQ (SAR 180M profit), Treasury (120M) and Procurement (90M) in Riyadh, Dubai or offshore, and set localisation 0–100%.
  • RHQ status (a game simplification) is worth SAR 95M a year once HQ sits in Riyadh at 60% localisation; extra points cost about 0.4M each.
  • An A: HQ in Riyadh, localisation at the bar, others offshore; moving them adds cost and saves no tax. All-offshore earns a D.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Assuming the tax holiday covers all Saudi profits.

✓ It covers qualifying RHQ activities; model other profits at normal rates and get advice.

✗ Moving every function to maximise the incentive.

✓ Move a function only if its own numbers beat its extra running and relocation cost.

✗ Counting the full contract pipeline as certain.

✓ Weight bids by a realistic win probability and work out the break-even rate.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1Which description of Saudi Arabia's RHQ incentive is most accurate?

The incentive needs a licensed RHQ with substance and covers only qualifying HQ activities. It excludes other profits, a sales office is not an RHQ, and it isn't a grant.

2An RHQ would hold SAR 100M of qualifying profit now taxed at 9%. Riyadh costs SAR 12M a year more; moving needs SAR 50M at 10% WACC. No contract upside. Net value?

Tax saved 100 × 9% = 9; capital charge 50 × 10% = 5; net 9 − 12 − 5 = −8. −3 forgets capital charge; +4 forgets running cost.

3Your regional HQ is moving to Riyadh, securing RHQ status. Treasury sits in a 0%-tax hub and would cost SAR 10M a year more in Riyadh. Should it move?

Treasury's tax cannot fall below 0% and the HQ already opens the contract door, so moving adds SAR 10M a year for nothing. 'Always' and 'never' are untested claims.

⚖️ The trade-off

Onshore commits you to a higher cost base for years, plus a hard senior hiring market. Staying in a cheaper hub keeps flexibility but closes the government-contract door. Relocation is hard to reverse: capital is spent and families move. You trade flexibility for access.

🧩 When the rule breaks

If the contract pipeline is speculative, the bridge can turn negative. Non-qualifying income is taxed normally, so get advice on what qualifies. A nameplate office fails substance tests. Rules evolve. And firms with no government customers and little regional profit should not move.

🏢 In real companies

Saudi Arabia launched the RHQ programme under Vision 2030, licensed through the Ministry of Investment. Since 2024, government contracting has favoured, and often required, a licensed RHQ, so firms with public-sector customers had to decide. The question is rarely 'move everything?' but 'which functions must be in Riyadh?'. Check current rules: conditions change.

💼 In an interview

“Should a multinational move its regional headquarters to Riyadh?”

It is a financial decision. The Saudi RHQ programme offers a long 0% tax holiday on qualifying income and access to government contracts, against a higher operating cost and local hiring requirements. I would model the tax saving plus contract upside against the extra cost and the substance requirements, function by function. Move what creates value in Riyadh; do not move for the headline.

📖 Key terms
Regional Headquarters (RHQ)
A licensed Saudi entity that manages a group's businesses across the region.
Tax holiday
A period when qualifying income is taxed at a reduced or zero rate.
Qualifying activities
The headquarters activities the incentive covers, under the programme's rules.
Substance
Real people and decisions located where the entity is registered.
Saudisation
Policies and expectations for employing Saudi nationals.
