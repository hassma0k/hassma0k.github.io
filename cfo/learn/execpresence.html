Credibility Is Capital

Board communication · credibility

5 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Earn the board's trust with candour on bad news, the right level of detail and an ask your credibility can carry.

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Trust is the cheapest capital you will ever raise, and the slowest to rebuild.
  • Lead with the bad news: the surprise costs more than the news.
  • Presence is calibration, not performance.
  • Ask for what your credibility can carry.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Explain why credibility is a financial asset that moves the cost of capital.
  • Structure bad news for a board: size, cause, plan, ask.
  • Calibrate candour, detail and ask size, and measure your forecast calibration.

💡The idea

Think of a weather forecaster. You don't trust one who always predicts sunshine, or one who forecasts storms daily to be safe. You trust the one whose '70% chance of rain' brings rain about seven days in ten. Boards judge CFOs the same way: by whether outcomes land where you said.

A board cannot check most of what you say, so it prices trust like a lender. Get three settings right. Candour: lead with bad news, sized, without alarm. Altitude: enough detail to show control, not so much the decision gets lost. The ask: big enough to show belief, small enough for your record to carry.

🧮The maths

Value and the cost of capital

Value = Invested capital + EVA ÷ WACC, where EVA = NOPAT − Invested capital × WACC

NOPAT
net operating profit after tax
WACC
cost of capital, including the risk premium demanded
EVA
profit left after charging for all capital used

Calibration rate

Calibration = Outcomes inside your stated range ÷ Forecasts given

Stated range
your range, with a confidence level such as 80%
Calibration
well calibrated when close to your stated confidence

✍️Worked example: Board day after a miss

Q3 margin missed plan by SAR 40M, and you want a SAR 300M plant. Invested capital is SAR 2,000M, NOPAT SAR 240M, WACC 9.0%. The draft deck hides the miss on slide 14 of 60 and asks for everything at once.

The working, step by step
  1. Lead with the miss: supplier prices 25 + late contract 15 = 40. About 15 returns in Q4; 25 needs pricing action.
  2. Altitude: one page with the bridge and the decision; the 60 slides become an appendix.
  3. Ask: approve SAR 300M in principle, release 120 now, tie the other 300 − 120 = 180 to two milestones.
  4. Baseline: EVA = 240 − 2,000 × 9% = 60; value = 2,000 + 60 ÷ 0.09 = 2,666.7.
  5. At 8.8% WACC: EVA = 240 − 2,000 × 8.8% = 64; value = 2,000 + 64 ÷ 0.088 = 2,727.3.

Answer. Lead with the miss, stay high-level, size the ask to your credibility. Trimming WACC 0.2 points through trust adds about SAR 60.6M.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Burying bad news late in the deck.

✓ Lead with size, cause, plan and ask. The surprise costs more than the news.

✗ Proving effort with detail.

✓ Present the decision at board altitude; put the analysis in an appendix.

✗ Asking big to leave room to negotiate.

✓ Ask for what the plan needs and your record can carry; gate large asks.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1Two weeks before the board meeting you find a SAR 30M shortfall against plan. What is the strongest approach?

Candour means early, sized and owned. Waiting or hiding turns a performance problem into a credibility problem; placing it after the good news reads as spin.

2For ten quarters your '80% confident' revenue ranges caught the actual all ten times. What does that suggest?

At 80%, about two in ten should miss. Ten of ten suggests padded ranges, or luck. Overconfidence shows up as too many misses, not too few.

3You need SAR 300M for a new plant, but you missed plan last quarter and trust is shaken. How do you size the ask?

A gated ask shows the whole commitment while releasing money as you deliver. Inflating is spin in reverse, slicing hides the total, and postponing can lose the opportunity.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Candour costs you on the day: a hard meeting, tougher questions. Spin buys calm and borrows against future credibility at a punishing rate. A gated ask trades speed for approval odds. A thin deck can look evasive, so offer the appendix.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Rooms differ: family boards may want more detail, investor boards headlines, crisis boards more candour. After a run of misses, even a sound bold ask is too much. Market-sensitive news must follow disclosure rules, and legal matters need counsel's wording.

🏢 In real companies

Gulf boards often mix founding families, government-linked investors and independent directors, and trust is built in private conversations too. Practise 'no surprises': brief the chair, and the audit committee chair where relevant, before bad news reaches the full board. For Saudi Exchange-listed companies, market-sensitive news follows disclosure rules, not the board calendar.

💼 In an interview

“How do you present bad news to the board?”

Early, candidly and with a plan. I state the issue and its size plainly, explain the cause, show the options with their financial impact, and give my recommendation. The right altitude matters — enough detail to show control, not so much that the message is lost. A CFO who never spins builds credibility, and credibility lets the board back you when you need a bold decision.

📖 Key terms
Candour
Stating bad news plainly and early, with its size, unprompted.
Altitude
The level of detail: strategic, operational or line by line.
Calibration
How closely your stated confidence matches how often you are right.
Gated ask
A funding request released in stages, each tied to a milestone.
Risk premium
Extra return investors demand for uncertainty; part of the cost of capital.
