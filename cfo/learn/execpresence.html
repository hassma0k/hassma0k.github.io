Credibility Is Capital

Board communication · credibility

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

How do you bring bad news and a big ask to a board that must trust you?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Trust is the cheapest capital you will raise, and slowest to rebuild.
  • Lead with bad news: the surprise costs more than the news.
  • Ask for what your credibility can carry.

💡The idea

You trust the weather forecaster whose '70% chance of rain' brings rain about seven days in ten, not one who always predicts sunshine or storms. Boards judge CFOs the same way: do outcomes land where you said?

A board cannot check most of what you say, so it prices trust like a lender. Set three things: candour (bad news first, sized), altitude (enough detail to show control) and the ask (bold enough to show belief, small enough for your record).

🧭Decide like a CFO: Board day after a miss

Margin missed plan last quarter, and you want a SAR 300M plant approved. The draft deck buries the miss deep in 60 slides and asks for everything.

Keep the draft → calm meeting today; when the miss surfaces, trust and approval odds fall.

Lead with the miss, ask for everything now → honest, but more than your record carries.

Lead with the miss, gate the ask on milestones → harder meeting, full plan shown, risk controlled.

The call. Option three: lead with the miss (size, cause, plan) on one page, then gate the ask. Earned trust trims WACC.

🧮The formula, in plain words

Value and the cost of capital

Value = Invested capital + EVA ÷ WACC

Trust lowers the risk premium in WACC, so the same profit is worth more.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Burying bad news late in the deck.

✓ Lead with size, cause, plan and ask; the surprise costs more.

✗ Proving effort with detail.

✓ Present the decision at board altitude; put analysis in an appendix.

✗ Asking big to leave room to negotiate.

✓ Ask for what the plan needs and your record carries; gate large asks.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1Two weeks before the board you find a SAR 30M shortfall against plan. What is the strongest approach?

Candour means early, sized and owned. Waiting or hiding turns a performance problem into a credibility problem.

2For ten quarters your '80% confident' revenue ranges caught every actual. What does that tell the board?

At 80%, about two in ten should miss. Ten of ten suggests padding; overconfidence shows as too many misses.

3You need SAR 300M for a plant, but you missed plan last quarter. How do you size the ask?

A gated ask shows the whole commitment while releasing money as you deliver. Inflating is spin; slicing hides the total.

⚖️ The trade-off

Candour costs you on the day: a hard meeting, tougher questions. Spin buys calm by borrowing against future credibility at a punishing rate. A gated ask trades speed for approval odds.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Rooms differ: family boards may want more detail, investor boards headlines. After a run of misses, even a sound bold ask is too much. Market-sensitive news follows disclosure rules; legal matters need counsel's wording.

🏢 In real companies

Gulf boards often mix founding families, government-linked investors and independents; trust is built privately too. Practise 'no surprises': brief the chair before the full board. On the Saudi Exchange, market-sensitive news follows disclosure rules, not the board calendar.

💼 In an interview

“How do you present bad news to the board?”

Early, candidly and with a plan. I state the issue and its size plainly, explain the cause, show the options with their financial impact, and give my recommendation. The right altitude matters — enough detail to show control, not so much that the message is lost. A CFO who never spins builds credibility, and credibility lets the board back you when you need a bold decision.

📖 Key terms
Candour
Stating bad news plainly and early, with its size, unprompted.
Altitude
The level of detail: strategic, operational or line by line.
Gated ask
A funding request released in stages, each tied to a milestone.
