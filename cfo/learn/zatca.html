Compliance That Pays You Back

Compliance as a cash lever · e-invoicing

5 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

How to run ZATCA’s Phase 2 e-invoicing as a collections project, not just compliance, and bring trapped cash home.

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Payment terms start from a valid invoice; DSO starts from the sale.
  • Compliance is the floor; cash is the prize.
  • Clearance checks the tax, not the deal.
  • Run e-invoicing with a DSO target, not just a go-live date.

💡The idea

Think of a parcel with a smudged address. It still ships, but bounces between depots until someone fixes the label. Phase 2 checks the label at the depot door: a faulty invoice is caught before it leaves, not three weeks later in the customer’s accounts-payable queue.

ZATCA, Saudi Arabia’s tax authority, is connecting invoicing systems to its Fatoora platform wave by wave (Phase 2). Business invoices are validated before the buyer sees them. Customers’ payment clocks start from a valid invoice, so prompt, clean invoices cut DSO (days customers take to pay). But clearance checks tax and format, not price.

🧮The maths

DSO in its parts

DSO ≈ payment terms + invoice lag + dispute days + days paid late

payment terms
days agreed in the contract
invoice lag
days from delivery until a valid invoice arrives
dispute days
extra days lost to rejected or re-issued invoices
days paid late
average days customers pay beyond terms

Cash released

Cash released = days of DSO removed × daily sales

daily sales
annual revenue ÷ 365

✍️Worked example: Where 84 days come from

An industrial supplier sells SAR 1,460M a year (SAR 4.0M a day) to businesses on 45-day terms. DSO is 84 days. Stock and supplier terms are out of scope. WACC is 9%, and its Phase 2 wave is approaching.

The working, step by step
  1. Break DSO down: 45 terms + 9 invoice lag + 16 disputes + 14 paid late = 84 days.
  2. Invoice at dispatch, not month-end: lag 9 → 1 (−8). Check VAT details and purchase orders first: disputes 16 → 6 (−10).
  3. Automatic reminders on clean invoices: late payment 14 → 10 (−4). New DSO = 45 + 1 + 6 + 10 = 62 days.
  4. Cash released = 22 days × SAR 4.0M = SAR 88M. Capital charge saved = 88 × 9% = SAR 7.92M a year.

Answer. SAR 88M was trapped in the company’s own invoicing, with terms unchanged. The mandate was the reason; the cash was the prize.

🎮How it plays in the game

  • Receivables have ballooned: sliders open at 84 days to collect, 58 of stock, 45 to pay — a 97-day cycle. Leaving them is a D.
  • ‘Collect from customers’ is the key lever: each day off DSO frees a day of sales, lifting EVA by that cash × WACC.
  • Below about 40 days to collect, customers walk and pushback grows fast. Moving stock towards 35 days and payment towards 60 adds the rest.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Running Phase 2 as an IT-only project

✓ Give finance joint ownership and a DSO target; redesign the whole invoice-to-cash process.

✗ Assuming a cleared invoice cannot be disputed

✓ Clearance checks tax and format; still match price, quantity and purchase orders before sending.

✗ Leaving data clean-up until the wave deadline

✓ Fix customer VAT numbers and master data early; go-live rejections cost compliance and cash.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1A contractor has DSO of 90 days on 60-day terms. Invoices go out 12 days after sign-off, and a third are rejected for missing details. Where does e-invoicing help most?

The 12-day lag and the rejection loops are self-inflicted, and validated, integrated invoicing attacks exactly those. ZATCA does not collect debts or set payment terms.

2After go-live, a customer disputes a cleared invoice: the unit price differs from its purchase order. What does this tell you?

ZATCA confirms a correct tax document; it cannot know the agreed price. Commercial accuracy is still the seller’s job. Assuming ‘cleared’ means ‘undisputable’ is the trap.

3Daily sales are SAR 2M. E-invoicing plus a collections redesign cuts DSO from 75 to 63 days. WACC is 8%. The redesign costs SAR 1.5M extra. Is it worth it?

12 × SAR 2M = SAR 24M released; 24 × 8% = SAR 1.92M every year receivables stay lower — covering SAR 1.5M within a year. It is cash, not profit.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Compliance is compulsory; the choice is how far beyond it to go. A bolt-on connector is cheaper but keeps month-end batching and the dispute backlog. The full redesign costs more — integration, data clean-up, training — but every self-inflicted day finances customers at your WACC.

🧩 When the rule breaks

E-invoicing fixes only the days you cause. If customers pay late because of long approval chains, a validated invoice will not help. Cash sales to consumers leave little DSO to win. Commercial disputes remain, and the switch can briefly hurt: gains arrive over quarters.

🏢 In real companies

E-invoicing applies to VAT-registered businesses resident in Saudi Arabia, reached wave by wave. Suppliers to large corporates and government-linked buyers gain most, because those buyers reject invoices that do not match exactly. Treat the wave notice as the start of a working-capital programme. Other GCC states are building similar schemes: design once, reuse.

💼 In an interview

“Is e-invoicing just a compliance cost?”

No, it can be a cash lever. ZATCA’s Phase-2 e-invoicing makes invoices structured, validated and real-time, which cuts disputes and rework. Done well, it shortens DSO and releases the working capital trapped in slow paper invoices. I would run it as a finance-transformation project with a collections target, not only an IT compliance deadline.

📖 Key terms
ZATCA
Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority: Saudi Arabia’s tax authority.
Fatoora
ZATCA’s e-invoicing platform, where Phase 2 invoices are cleared or reported.
Clearance
ZATCA validates a business-to-business tax invoice before the buyer receives it.
Reporting
Simplified consumer invoices are sent to ZATCA after they are issued.
Invoice-to-cash
The chain from delivery to cash: invoicing, matching, disputes and collection.
