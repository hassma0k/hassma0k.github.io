Compliance That Pays You Back

Compliance as a cash lever · e-invoicing

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

Will you run ZATCA Phase 2 e-invoicing as mere compliance, or as a cash-collection programme?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Compliance is the floor; cash is the prize.
  • Clearance checks the tax, not the deal.
  • Run e-invoicing with a DSO target, not just a go-live date.

💡The idea

Think of a parcel with a smudged address. It still ships, but bounces between depots until someone fixes the label. Phase 2 checks the label at the door, before the invoice reaches the customer.

ZATCA is connecting invoicing systems to its Fatoora platform wave by wave, validating business invoices before buyers see them. Payment clocks usually start from a valid invoice, so prompt, clean invoicing cuts DSO and releases cash. Clearance checks tax and format, not price.

🧭Decide like a CFO: Where 84 days come from

An industrial supplier sells to large businesses on 45-day terms, but DSO is 84 days. Invoices go out at month-end and many are rejected. Its Phase 2 wave is approaching.

Bolt-on connector only → compliant at least cost, but month-end batching and rejections keep cash trapped.

Redesign invoice-to-cash: invoice at dispatch, match purchase orders → most self-inflicted days disappear, at higher cost.

Demand shorter customer terms → quick on paper, but strains key accounts and ignores the real cause.

The call. Redesign the process. The mandate is the reason; the cash trapped in your own invoicing is the prize, with terms unchanged.

🧮The formula, in plain words

DSO in its parts

DSO ≈ payment terms + invoice lag + dispute days + days paid late

Only terms are agreed; invoice lag and disputes are largely self-inflicted days you can remove.

🎮How it plays in the game

  • Receivables have ballooned: sliders open at 84 days to collect, 58 of stock, 45 to pay.
  • ‘Collect from customers’ is the key lever: each day off DSO lifts EVA. Untouched sliders score a D.
  • Below 40 days to collect, pushback grows fast; stock towards 35 and payment towards 60 add the rest.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Running Phase 2 as an IT-only project

✓ Give finance joint ownership and a DSO target, not just a go-live date.

✗ Assuming a cleared invoice cannot be disputed

✓ Clearance checks tax and format; still match price and purchase orders first.

✗ Leaving data clean-up until the wave deadline

✓ Fix customer VAT numbers and master data early; go-live rejections cost cash.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1DSO is well above terms. Invoices go out days after delivery and a third are rejected. Where does e-invoicing help most?

Lag and rejections are self-inflicted days, exactly what validated, integrated invoicing attacks. ZATCA neither collects debts nor sets terms.

2After go-live, a customer disputes a cleared invoice because the price differs from its purchase order. What does this tell you?

ZATCA confirms a valid tax document; it cannot know the agreed price. Commercial accuracy remains the seller’s job.

3The board asks whether to fund a full invoice-to-cash redesign beyond the minimum compliant connector. What decides it?

Each self-inflicted day removed releases cash and saves its capital charge every year. Weigh that against the extra spend.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Compliance is compulsory; the choice is how far beyond it to go. A bolt-on connector is cheaper but keeps the dispute backlog. A full redesign costs more, yet every self-inflicted day finances customers at WACC.

🧩 When the rule breaks

E-invoicing fixes only the days you cause, not customers’ slow approval chains or genuine commercial disputes. Cash sales to consumers leave little DSO to win. The switch can briefly hurt; gains arrive over quarters.

🏢 In real companies

E-invoicing applies to VAT-registered businesses resident in Saudi Arabia, reached wave by wave. Suppliers to large corporates and government-linked buyers gain most, because those buyers reject mismatched invoices. Other GCC states are building similar schemes: design once, reuse.

💼 In an interview

“Is e-invoicing just a compliance cost?”

No, it can be a cash lever. ZATCA’s Phase-2 e-invoicing makes invoices structured, validated and real-time, which cuts disputes and rework. Done well, it shortens DSO and releases the working capital trapped in slow paper invoices. I would run it as a finance-transformation project with a collections target, not only an IT compliance deadline.

📖 Key terms
