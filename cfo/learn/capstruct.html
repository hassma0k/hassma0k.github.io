The Leverage Line

Trade-off theory of capital structure

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

How much debt should you carry: enough for cheap money, not enough to break the rating?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • Debt is cheap until the rating breaks.
  • Size debt for the bad year, not the average one.
  • All-equity is not free safety; it is expensive safety.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Explain why WACC falls, then rises, with debt.
  • Weigh the tax shield against rating and distress risk.
  • Judge a target debt range that survives a downturn.

💡The idea

Think of a tower built floor by floor. Debt floors are cheap, so it rises fast, but each adds sway in the wind. Too short wastes cheap floors; too tall is fine until a storm.

Debt is cheaper than equity: lenders are paid first and interest is tax-deductible. So moderate debt lowers WACC and lifts value. But more debt thins interest cover, the rating slips and everyone charges more. The bottom is flat, then a cliff: stay slightly safe.

🧭Decide like a CFO: Three ways to fund growth

A stable company funds itself with 20% debt and an AA rating. The board weighs moving to a mid-range A-rated mix or 65% debt for a large buyback.

Stay at 20% → safe AA rating, but a higher WACC every year: safety paid out of sight.

Move to around 40–45% → lowest WACC; rating slips to A, still investment grade with headroom.

Go to 65% → bigger tax shield, but a B rating re-prices debt and equity; WACC rises.

The call. Choose about 40–45%: lowest WACC at investment grade, with headroom. The bigger shield at 65% cannot buy back the lost rating.

🧮The formula, in plain words

Weighted average cost of capital

WACC = E/V × Ke + D/V × Kd × (1 − t)

The blended yearly return owners and lenders require, with debt cost cut by its tax saving.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Judging debt by its interest rate alone

✓ More debt raises the cost of equity, then debt; judge the whole WACC.

✗ Sizing debt on this year’s profit

✓ Stress-test cover and rating for a downturn and rate shock.

✗ Assuming every riyal of interest saves tax

✓ Check losses, zakat and tax holidays; each changes the real shield.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1An AA-rated company with stable profit could raise debt for a buyback and drop to A. What happens to WACC?

At moderate leverage the tax shield and cheap debt outweigh extra risk. The cost of equity actually rises with debt.

2Pushing debt deep into sub-investment grade nearly triples the tax shield, yet WACC rises. Why?

The shield is real cash, but the rating drops, all debt re-prices and owners demand more. Distress costs win.

3Two firms are identical except one has steady contracted cash flows, the other volatile commodity earnings. Which should carry more debt?

Distress risk is the chance cash falls short of interest. Steady cash makes that less likely, so it can carry more debt.

⚖️ The trade-off

Debt buys a lower WACC with flexibility: it uses up headroom for downturns and rate shocks, and covenants can restrict dividends. Too safe also costs: an all-equity company pays a higher WACC every year.

🧩 When the rule breaks

The theory explains the shape, not the number. Loss-makers gain little tax shield. Stable utilities can carry leverage that would sink a cyclical tech firm. Ratings are judgements; the game simplifies them into clean bands.

🏢 In real companies

Boards usually set a target rating or a ratio such as net debt to EBITDA. Much GCC borrowing is Sharia-compliant, such as sukuk, but the logic holds. Saudi zakat and income tax apply by ownership, so shields differ.

💼 In an interview

“How do you decide how much debt the company should carry?”

Trade-off theory. Debt is cheaper than equity because interest is tax-deductible, so some leverage lowers WACC. But each extra notch raises the probability and cost of financial distress — rating downgrades, higher spreads, covenants. I target the range where the tax shield still outweighs distress cost, keep headroom for a downturn, and check it against our rating target and cash-flow volatility.

📖 Key terms
Tax shield
Tax saved because interest is deductible.
Interest cover
Operating profit ÷ interest: how many times profit covers interest.
Investment grade
A credit rating of BBB or above.
