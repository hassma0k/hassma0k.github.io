Which AI Actually Pays

Positive-NPV rule applied to automation

3 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

One automation budget, six AI workflows: which earn more than their capital costs, and in what order?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • AI is capital allocation in a new suit.
  • Boring workflows that pay back fast beat glamorous programmes.
  • Hours saved are not cash until cost leaves.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Judge an AI pitch as a capital project.
  • Weigh automations by value per unit of delivery capacity.
  • Spot the “replatform everything” programme that destroys value.

💡The idea

Fixing a leaking pipe is cheap and quick, and the saving shows on next month’s bill. Rebuilding the house to fix it ties up huge sums for years. Finance’s leaking pipes: invoice matching, collections, reconciliations, document extraction.

An AI project is a capital project: its net annual return must beat the cost of capital. Money and the few people who deliver change are both scarce, so rank by value per effort. Hours saved count only when cost leaves or time earns.

🧭Decide like a CFO: Quick wins or the ERP?

You have SAR 420M and a small delivery team. Bids range from invoice automation to a full ERP replatform that returns less than capital costs. A board member champions the ERP.

Lead with the ERP → absorbs most capacity, destroys value, leaves room for one quick win.

Include the GenAI copilot → it fits, but crowds out wins worth more per person.

Rank by value per effort point → three workflow automations, most value, cash left to return.

The call. Fund AP/AR, close automation and document extraction: most value per scarce person. Return unspent cash; queue the copilot.

🧮The formula, in plain words

Annual automation return

Return = (Annual benefits − New running costs) ÷ Investment

Real savings minus new running costs, per riyal invested, must beat the cost of capital.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Counting hours saved as cash saved.

✓ Name where it lands: contract ended, hire avoided, or time redeployed.

✗ Leaving out the new running costs.

✓ Net off licences, usage fees, support and human review.

✗ Ranking by return while ignoring delivery capacity.

✓ When the same few people deliver everything, rank by value per effort.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1A GenAI tool saves thousands of hours a year, but no roles change and nobody is redeployed. How should you value it?

Hours saved are value only if cost leaves or time earns. Meanwhile, the running costs are real.

2Your delivery team is nearly full. One large project would add the most value but doesn’t fit. What do you do?

A project that does not fit delivers nothing. Cutting scope needs a new business case, and stretched teams slip.

3A board member backs a large ERP replatform returning below WACC: “Small automations are just patches.” Strongest CFO response?

This case destroys value, but replatforming is not always wrong. Ask for retired legacy cost or risk removed.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Ranking means saying “not yet” to projects that clear the hurdle, like the copilot. Small automations also leave old systems in place, postponing a modernisation you may eventually need.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Vendor estimates are optimistic, pilots use cleaner data than production, and adoption is never automatic. A replatform can be right if the old system is failing or non-compliant. Pilot, measure, then re-decide.

🏢 In real companies

Finance teams start with payables, receivables, reconciliations and the close. In Saudi Arabia, ZATCA’s Phase-2 e-invoicing creates structured invoice data, so plan it with AP/AR automation. Tools must handle Arabic and English, and the Personal Data Protection Law applies.

💼 In an interview

“How would you decide which AI and automation projects to fund?”

Like any capital project: return above the hurdle rate. I would fund the highest-ROI, fastest-payback workflows first — invoice processing, reconciliations, the close — prove the savings, then scale. A large “replatform everything” programme with a return below our cost of capital destroys value, however modern it sounds. Sequence by ROI and execution capacity, and measure benefits after go-live.

📖 Key terms
Execution capacity
The limited people and attention available for projects at once.
Replatform
Replacing a core system, such as the ERP, wholesale.
Benefits realisation
Checking after go-live that promised savings reached the numbers.
