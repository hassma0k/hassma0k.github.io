Which AI Actually Pays

Positive-NPV rule applied to automation

6 min read · 3-question self-check · practise it in the app

One automation budget, six AI workflows: which earn more than the capital they use, and in what order?

⚡In 30 seconds

  • AI is capital allocation in a new suit: beat the cost of capital.
  • The boring workflow that pays back fast beats the glamorous programme that might.
  • When people are the bottleneck, rank by value per unit of their time.
  • Hours saved are not cash until cost leaves or the time earns.

🎯What you’ll learn

  • Turn an automation pitch into an annual return and test it against the hurdle.
  • Sequence automation projects when both budget and delivery people are limited.
  • Spot the big “replatform everything” programme that destroys value.

💡The idea

Fixing a leaking pipe is cheap and quick, and the saving shows on next month’s bill. Rebuilding the house to fix the leak ties up huge sums for years before anything pays back. In finance, the leaking pipes are invoice matching, collections, reconciliations and document extraction: high volume, clear rules, structured data.

An AI project is a capital project: (annual benefits − new running costs) ÷ investment must beat the WACC (weighted average cost of capital). Two things are scarce: money and the few people who can deliver change, so rank by value per effort point. And hours saved count only when cost actually leaves or the freed time earns something.

🧮The maths

Annual automation return

Return = (Annual benefits − New running costs) ÷ Investment

Annual benefits
costs removed, errors avoided, cash collected sooner
New running costs
licences, usage fees, support and oversight
Investment
build, integration, data preparation and change management

Value per unit of delivery capacity

Value density = EVA ÷ Effort

EVA
(Return − WACC) × Investment, each year
Effort
share of your scarce delivery team needed

✍️Worked example: Sequencing six automation bids

WACC 8.4%, budget SAR 420M, 7 effort points. AP/AR automation: 90M, 22%, effort 2. Close automation: 110M, 18%, 3. Document extraction: 70M, 16%, 1. Forecast copilot: 130M, 15%, 4. Service agents: 80M, 11%, 2. ERP replatform: 250M, 4%, 5.

The working, step by step
  1. Spread over 8.4%: AP/AR 13.6 pts, Close 9.6, Extraction 7.6, Copilot 6.6, Agents 2.6, ERP 4 − 8.4 = −4.4.
  2. EVA (SAR M a year): AP/AR 90 × 13.6% = 12.24; Close 10.56; Extraction 5.32; Copilot 8.58; Agents 2.08; ERP −11.00.
  3. The five creators need 12 points against 7. Rank by EVA per point: AP/AR 6.12, Extraction 5.32, Close 3.52, Copilot 2.15.
  4. Top three: 6 points, SAR 270M, EVA 12.24 + 5.32 + 10.56 = 28.12. AP/AR + Extraction + Copilot (7 points): 26.14.
  5. ERP-led plan: replatform uses 5 points, leaving room for AP/AR only. EVA −11.00 + 12.24 = 1.24.

Answer. Fund AP/AR, close automation and document extraction (SAR 28.12M EVA a year), return the unspent SAR 150M, and queue the copilot.

⚠️Common mistakes

✗ Counting hours saved as cash saved.

✓ Name where it lands: contract ended, hire avoided, overtime cut, or time redeployed to revenue work.

✗ Leaving out the running costs.

✓ Net off licences, usage fees, support and human review; they can push a project below the hurdle.

✗ Ranking by return while ignoring delivery capacity.

✓ When the same few people deliver everything, rank by value per effort point and compare whole slates.

✅Self-check

Three questions. Tap an answer to see why.

1A SAR 40M GenAI tool saves 30,000 hours a year, valued by the vendor at SAR 6M. No roles change; nobody is redeployed. Running costs: SAR 1M a year. Honest annual return?

Hours saved are value only if cost leaves or the time earns. The cash benefit is near zero, while the 1M running cost is real. 12.5% uses the wrong benefit.

2Three effort points left. Option A: one project, EVA SAR 8.58M a year, needs 4 points. Option B: two projects, EVA 5.32M (1 point) and 2.08M (2 points). What do you fund?

Option A does not fit, so its 8.58M is not available. B fits and adds 5.32 + 2.08 = 7.40M. A cut scope needs a new business case.

3A board member backs a SAR 250M ERP replatform returning 4% against an 8.4% WACC: “It’s the future; small automations are just patches.” Strongest CFO response?

250 × (4% − 8.4%) = −11.0M a year. Replatforming is not always wrong; this case lacks the value. Ask for retired legacy cost or risk removed; return unspent capital.

🔎Go deeper

⚖️ The trade-off

Ranking means saying “not yet” to projects that clear the hurdle: the copilot adds SAR 8.58M a year and still misses the cut. Small automations also leave old systems in place, postponing a modernisation you may eventually need.

🧩 When the rule breaks

Vendor estimates are optimistic, pilots run on cleaner data than production, and adoption is never automatic. A replatform can be right if the old system is failing or cannot meet a regulation. Usage pricing can grow with success. Pilot, measure, then re-run the numbers.

🏢 In real companies

Finance teams start with payables, receivables, reconciliations and the month-end close. In Saudi Arabia, Phase-2 e-invoicing from ZATCA, the tax authority, produces structured invoice data, so plan e-invoicing and AP/AR automation together. Tools must handle Arabic and English documents, and personal data falls under the Personal Data Protection Law, which can add cost and time.

💼 In an interview

“How would you decide which AI and automation projects to fund?”

Like any capital project: return above the hurdle rate. I would fund the highest-ROI, fastest-payback workflows first — invoice processing, reconciliations, the close — prove the savings, then scale. A large “replatform everything” programme with a return below our cost of capital destroys value, however modern it sounds. Sequence by ROI and execution capacity, and measure benefits after go-live.

📖 Key terms
Automation ROI
Annual net benefit of an automation divided by its build cost.
Payback period
Years of net benefits needed to recover the investment.
Execution capacity
The limited people and management attention available for projects at once.
Replatform
Replacing a core system, such as the ERP, wholesale.
Benefits realisation
Checking after go-live that promised savings actually reached the numbers.
